06 / FORMULATION & EVIDENCE

What is inside,
and what supports it.

Every benefit statement below is deliberately separated into traditional evidence and modern human research. We do not present an Ayurvedic description as clinical proof.

01ASHWAGANDHA

Withania somnifera

Formulation reference: Ashwagandha root is the traditional botanical identity used in many Ayurvedic preparations. The commercial TN formula should disclose root/extract ratio, extraction solvent and withanolide standardisation before a quantitative claim is made.

Traditional reference

Classical Ayurvedic literature describes Ashwagandha in Rasayana/Balya contexts associated with nourishment and strength.

Effect described traditionally: strengthening, nourishment and Rasayana support.

Scientific review of classical sources ↗
Human evidence / studied effects

Some standardized preparations have been studied for perceived stress and sleep outcomes. Effects are preparation- and dose-dependent, so this page does not promise a specific result for the TN product.

NCCIH evidence & safety ↗
Evidence status: preparation-specific • not a disease-treatment claim
02HARIDRA / TURMERIC

Curcuma longa

Formulation reference: Turmeric rhizome is the botanical source. If the product is called “curcumin,” the label should state the curcuminoid content and any bioavailability technology rather than implying that all turmeric contains the same dose.

Traditional reference / described effects

Ayurvedic sources describe Haridra in traditional contexts including skin and digestive applications.

Effect described traditionally: use in formulations associated with skin and digestive care.

Classical-reference catalogue ↗
Human evidence / studied effects

Human trials have mainly examined pain and function in osteoarthritis and other inflammatory outcomes. Some results are promising, but products differ in curcuminoid content and absorption.

NCCIH evidence & safety ↗
Safety note: highly bioavailable formulations have reported liver-injury cases.
03TRIPHALA

Amalaki + Haritaki + Bibhitaki

Formulation reference: Triphala is a three-fruit Ayurvedic formulation: Amalaki, Haritaki and Bibhitaki. Classical preparations and ratios can differ; TN must publish its actual ratio and dose on the final specification/label.

Traditional reference / described effects

Triphala is documented in the classical Ayurvedic tradition as a Rasayana formulation.

Effect described traditionally: support within Rasayana and digestive-use traditions.

Review of traditional & scientific literature ↗
Human evidence / studied effects

Human evidence remains limited. The cited Phase I study primarily evaluated safety, so it should not be used as evidence that Triphala produces a broad “detox” or digestive-health effect.

PubMed safety study ↗
Evidence status: limited human evidence • do not overstate “detox” claims
04BRAHMI

Bacopa monnieri

Identity control: “Brahmi” can refer to different plants in different traditions. The product specification should name the botanical species, plant part, extract ratio and standardised marker compound.

Traditional reference / described effects

Classical Ayurvedic sources place Brahmi in Medhya Rasayana traditions associated with cognition and mental function.

Effect described traditionally: support for memory, learning and mental function.

Classical-reference guide ↗
Human evidence / studied effects

Randomized-trial reviews have examined Bacopa for memory and attention, with some evidence of improvement in selected cognitive measures after sustained use. Results depend on extract, dose and study design.

Human clinical-trial review ↗
Evidence status: cognitive evidence is preparation/dose dependent
05MORINGA

Moringa oleifera

Formulation reference: For a powder product, disclose the exact plant part (for example, leaf), processing method and serving weight. “Superfood” is a marketing category, not a scientific endpoint.

Traditional reference / described effects

Ayurvedic sources identify Moringa as Shigru and record traditional uses in classical materia-medica references.

Effect described traditionally: use as a botanical food/medicine in traditional materia-medica.

Classical-reference catalogue ↗
Human evidence / studied effects

Human studies have explored nutritional and metabolic outcomes, but the evidence is still limited and heterogeneous. Moringa should be presented here primarily as a nutrient-containing food powder, not a treatment.

Systematic review ↗
Evidence status: nutritional evidence • not a treatment claim

Important source note

These product-specific botanical references are from classical Ayurvedic texts, not claims that the Vedas or Puranas prescribe modern capsules or extracts. Where a Vedic/Puranic source does not directly establish a formulation, the site does not attribute it to one.

Our evidence standard

Classical text ≠ clinical proof. Traditional Ayurvedic descriptions are identified as traditional references. Modern benefits are described only at the level supported by human research, and uncertainty is shown where evidence is limited.
